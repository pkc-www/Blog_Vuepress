Types and construction

  • Core-form: consists a simple rectangular laminated piece of steel with the transformers windings wrapped around two sides.
  • Shell-form: consists three legs laminated core with winding wrapped around the center leg.
  • Primary winding or input winding: The winding connected to the power source.
  • Secondary winding or output winding: The winding connected to the loads.
  • Core material and eddy current: The core is constructed of thin laminations electrically isolated from each other in order to minimize the eddy current.

§ Transformer Taps and Voltage Regulation

  • Transformer Taps: Taps are intermediate connection points on the transformer windings used to change the effective turns ratio aaa. When the utility line voltage fluctuates, changing the taps helps maintain a stable output voltage.
  • TCUL (Tap Changing Under Load): A specialized transformer capable of changing its tap connections while the power is connected and the transformer is under load.
  • Voltage Regulator: A TCUL equipped with automatic voltage sensing circuitry that changes taps dynamically to maintain the output voltage at a predefined level.

§ The Autotransformer

  • Autotransformer: A transformer in which the primary and secondary windings share a common physical winding.
    • Allows continuous tuning of the output voltage magnitude.
    • The physical size (and cost) of an autotransformer is smaller than a conventional two-winding transformer of the same power rating.
    • There is no electrical isolation between the primary and secondary sides.

The Step-Up Connection of Autotransformer (AT):

An autotransformer has one continuous winding divided into:

  • Common winding with NCN_CNC​ turns.
  • Series winding with NSEN_{SE}NSE​ turns.

§ Review of Three-Phase Power

Three-phase systems have two main connection types: Wye (Y\text{Y}Y) and Delta (Δ\DeltaΔ).

  • Line voltage (VLV_LVL​): The voltage between any two line conductors (VABV_{AB}VAB​, VBCV_{BC}VBC​, VCAV_{CA}VCA​).

  • Phase voltage (VϕV_\phiVϕ​): The voltage across a single phase winding (VANV_{AN}VAN​, VBNV_{BN}VBN​, VCNV_{CN}VCN​).

Wye (Y\text{Y}Y) connection:

  • Line voltage is 3\sqrt{3}3​ times phase voltage: VL=3VϕV_L = \sqrt{3} V_\phiVL​=3​Vϕ​

  • Line current equals phase current: IL=IϕI_L = I_\phiIL​=Iϕ​

§ Three-Phase Transformer Connections

A three-phase transformer bank can be connected in four ways:

1) Wye-Wye (Y-Y) Connection

  • The line voltage ratio is:

    VLPVLS=3VϕP3VϕS=a\frac{V_{LP}}{V_{LS}} = \frac{\sqrt{3} V_{\phi P}}{\sqrt{3} V_{\phi S}} = a VLS​VLP​​=3​VϕS​3​VϕP​​=a

注意

  1. If the loads are unbalanced, the neutral point will shift, causing severe unbalance in phase voltages.
  2. The third-harmonic voltage component can be very large.

重要

Why is the third-harmonic voltage large?

The third-harmonic magnetizing currents in all three phases are in-phase (3(ωt−120∘)=3ωt3(\omega t - 120^\circ) = 3\omega t3(ωt−120∘)=3ωt). Being zero-sequence components, they require a return path to flow. In an ungrounded Y-Y\text{Y-Y}Y-Y connection, they cannot flow, distorting the core flux and inducing large third-harmonic voltages.

Solutions:

  1. Solidly ground the neutral of the primary winding to the source neutral, providing a path for the third-harmonic currents.
  2. Add a Δ\DeltaΔ-connected tertiary winding. The third-harmonic currents circulate inside the closed Δ\DeltaΔ loop, suppressing the harmonic voltage.

重要

Why Δ\DeltaΔ is special?

With a Delta connection, we do not need a neutral line to release the third-harmonic current. Its physical loop construction allows third-harmonic currents to circulate and be confined within the loop.

2) Wye-Delta (Y-Δ\text{Y-}\DeltaY-Δ) Connection:

  • The line voltage ratio is:

    VLPVLS=3VϕPVϕS=3a\frac{V_{LP}}{V_{LS}} = \frac{\sqrt{3} V_{\phi P}}{V_{\phi S}} = \sqrt{3} a VLS​VLP​​=VϕS​3​VϕP​​=3​a

  • There is a 30∘30^\circ30∘ phase shift between primary and secondary sides. Typically, the secondary line voltage lags the primary line voltage by 30∘30^\circ30∘.
  • Because of the closed Delta winding, we do not have third-harmonic components in Wye-Delta.
  • In the United States, the standard convention is that the secondary voltage lags the primary voltage by 30∘30^\circ30∘.

3) Delta-Wye (Δ-Y\Delta\text{-Y}Δ-Y) Connection:

  • The line voltage ratio is:

    VLPVLS=VϕP3VϕS=a3\frac{V_{LP}}{V_{LS}} = \frac{V_{\phi P}}{\sqrt{3} V_{\phi S}} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{3}} VLS​VLP​​=3​VϕS​VϕP​​=3​a​

Delta-Wye Connection Applications

  • Common Applications:
    • Widely used for step-down distribution (transforming a 3-phase 3-wire line to a 3-phase 4-wire line, providing a neutral for single-phase loads).
    • Used to isolate the ground of the Wye side from the source ground on the Delta side.

Summary:

  • Δ-Y\Delta\text{-Y}Δ-Y: Common for step-down distribution. Low-voltage side is grounded Wye (3-phase 4-wire). High-voltage Delta isolates zero-sequence currents.
  • Y-Δ\text{Y-}\DeltaY-Δ: Common for step-up generation or interconnection. High-voltage side is grounded Wye. Protection & Reference

  • Line voltage ratio:

    VLPVLS=a\frac{V_{LP}}{V_{LS}} = a VLS​VLP​​=a

注

Δ-Δ\Delta\text{-}\DeltaΔ-Δ Connection is rarely used because it does not have a neutral line to provide necessary protection for operation.

§ Parallel Operation of Transformers

Advantages of parallel operation:

  • Flexible operation facing load changes, improving overall efficiency.
  • Cost-effective (easier to replace or maintain smaller units).
  • Highly expandable for future capacity planning.

Ideal parallel operation conditions:

  • No circulating currents under no-load conditions.
  • Each unit shares the load in proportion to its power rating.

§ How to Achieve Parallel Operation

To achieve ideal parallel operation:

  1. Same voltage rating (turns ratio must be equal; difference should be <0.5%< 0.5\%<0.5%).
  2. Same phase angle shift between primary and secondary (same connection group).
  3. Short-circuit impedance (per-unit impedance) should be similar to ensure equal load sharing.
