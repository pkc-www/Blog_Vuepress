第3章 交流电机基础 AC Machinery Fundamentals

Avalanche2026/3/21大约 7 分钟电机学电机课程笔记英文

AC machines can be divided into two major classes:

  • Synchronous machines: The magnetic field is generated by a rotor supplied with DC current (or permanent magnets), and the rotor rotates at synchronous speed.
  • Asynchronous (Induction) machines: The rotor magnetic field is induced by electromagnetic induction from the stator currents. The rotor always rotates slower than the synchronous speed.

§ Simple Loop in a Uniform Magnetic Field

To understand AC generators, we analyze a simple rotating loop in a uniform magnetic field:

According to Faraday's law of induced voltage on a moving wire:

eind=(v⃗×B⃗)⋅l⃗e_{ind} = (\vec{v} \times \vec{B}) \cdot \vec{l} eind​=(v×B)⋅l

For the rotating loop of radius rrr and length lll rotating at angular speed ω\omegaω:

  • Segment ab: The velocity vector v⃗\vec{v}v is directed at angle θ\thetaθ to the B⃗\vec{B}B field.

    eba=vBlsin⁡θab(directed into the page)e_{ba} = v B l \sin\theta_{ab} \quad (\text{directed into the page}) eba​=vBlsinθab​(directed into the page)

  • Segment cd:

    edc=vBlsin⁡θcd(directed out of the page)e_{dc} = v B l \sin\theta_{cd} \quad (\text{directed out of the page}) edc​=vBlsinθcd​(directed out of the page)

  • Segments bc and ad: Since the velocity is parallel to the magnetic field along these segments, the induced voltage is zero.

    ebc=ead=0e_{bc} = e_{ad} = 0 ebc​=ead​=0

The total induced voltage around the loop is:

eind=eba+edc=2vBlsin⁡θe_{ind} = e_{ba} + e_{dc} = 2 v B l \sin\theta eind​=eba​+edc​=2vBlsinθ

Using the relationships v=rωv = r \omegav=rω and the loop area A=2rlA = 2 r lA=2rl, we get:

eind=2rωBlsin⁡ωt=ABωsin⁡ωte_{ind} = 2 r \omega B l \sin\omega t = A B \omega \sin\omega t eind​=2rωBlsinωt=ABωsinωt

Since the maximum flux through the loop is Φmax=AB\Phi_{max} = A BΦmax​=AB, the induced voltage can be written as:

eind=Φmaxωsin⁡ωte_{ind} = \Phi_{max} \omega \sin\omega t eind​=Φmax​ωsinωt

§ Induced Torque in a Current-Carrying Loop

When the loop carries a current iii, a magnetic force is exerted on the wire segments:

F⃗=i(l⃗×B⃗)\vec{F} = i (\vec{l} \times \vec{B}) F=i(l×B)

The torque on each segment is:

τ=F⋅rsin⁡θ\tau = F \cdot r \sin\theta τ=F⋅rsinθ

  • Segment ab:

    τab=rilBsin⁡θab(clockwise)\tau_{ab} = r i l B \sin\theta_{ab} \quad (\text{clockwise}) τab​=rilBsinθab​(clockwise)

  • Segment cd:

    τcd=rilBsin⁡θcd(clockwise)\tau_{cd} = r i l B \sin\theta_{cd} \quad (\text{clockwise}) τcd​=rilBsinθcd​(clockwise)

  • Segments bc and ad: Induced torque is zero.

    τbc=τad=0\tau_{bc} = \tau_{ad} = 0 τbc​=τad​=0

The total induced torque is:

τind=2rilBsin⁡θ\tau_{ind} = 2 r i l B \sin\theta τind​=2rilBsinθ

This can be alternatively expressed in vector form using the loop's magnetic moment:

τ⃗ind=kB⃗R×B⃗S\vec{\tau}_{ind} = k \vec{B}_R \times \vec{B}_S τind​=kBR​×BS​

where B⃗R\vec{B}_RBR​ is the rotor-produced flux density and B⃗S\vec{B}_SBS​ is the stator-produced flux density.

§ Rotating Magnetic Field

In real AC machines, instead of a rotating loop in a stationary magnetic field, we have a rotating magnetic field (RMF) interacting with stationary windings (stator).

To generate a rotating magnetic field, we place three coils spaced 120∘120^\circ120∘ electrical degrees apart on the stator and supply them with balanced three-phase currents:

ia(t)=Imcos⁡ωti_a(t) = I_m \cos\omega t ia​(t)=Im​cosωt

ib(t)=Imcos⁡(ωt−120∘)i_b(t) = I_m \cos(\omega t - 120^\circ) ib​(t)=Im​cos(ωt−120∘)

ic(t)=Imcos⁡(ωt−240∘)i_c(t) = I_m \cos(\omega t - 240^\circ) ic​(t)=Im​cos(ωt−240∘)

  • Armature winding: Located on the stator, used to collect electrical power (generator) or supply power (motor).
  • Field winding: Located on the rotor, supplied with DC current to generate the main magnetic field.

§ Induced Voltage on a Three-Phase Stator

Assuming the rotor produces a sinusoidally distributed flux density:

B(θ)=Bmcos⁡θB(\theta) = B_m \cos\theta B(θ)=Bm​cosθ

As the rotor rotates at electrical speed ω\omegaω:

B(θ,t)=Bmcos⁡(ωt−θ)B(\theta, t) = B_m \cos(\omega t - \theta) B(θ,t)=Bm​cos(ωt−θ)

The total induced voltage in an NcN_cNc​-turn stator coil is:

eind(t)=NcΦmaxωsin⁡ωte_{ind}(t) = N_c \Phi_{max} \omega \sin\omega t eind​(t)=Nc​Φmax​ωsinωt

For three coils spaced 120∘120^\circ120∘ apart, the induced voltages are:

eaa′(t)=Emaxsin⁡ωte_{aa'}(t) = E_{max} \sin\omega t eaa′​(t)=Emax​sinωt

ebb′(t)=Emaxsin⁡(ωt−120∘)e_{bb'}(t) = E_{max} \sin(\omega t - 120^\circ) ebb′​(t)=Emax​sin(ωt−120∘)

ecc′(t)=Emaxsin⁡(ωt−240∘)e_{cc'}(t) = E_{max} \sin(\omega t - 240^\circ) ecc′​(t)=Emax​sin(ωt−240∘)

The RMS phase voltage is:

Erms=Emax2=NcΦmaxω2=2πfNcΦmax≈4.44fNcΦmaxE_{rms} = \frac{E_{max}}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{N_c \Phi_{max} \omega}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2} \pi f N_c \Phi_{max} \approx 4.44 f N_c \Phi_{max} Erms​=2​Emax​​=2​Nc​Φmax​ω​=2​πfNc​Φmax​≈4.44fNc​Φmax​

§ Three-Phase Stator Connections and Harmonics

  • Wye (Y) Connection:

    • Line-to-line voltage is VL=3VϕV_L = \sqrt{3} V_\phiVL​=3​Vϕ​.
    • The third-harmonic voltages (3ωt3\omega t3ωt, 3(ωt−120∘)=3ωt3(\omega t - 120^\circ) = 3\omega t3(ωt−120∘)=3ωt) in each phase are in-phase (zero-sequence). Because of this, the third harmonics cancel out in the line-to-line voltage:

      eAB,3=eA,3−eB,3=0e_{AB, 3} = e_{A, 3} - e_{B, 3} = 0 eAB,3​=eA,3​−eB,3​=0

    • Benefit: Line-to-line voltage contains no third-harmonic components.

  • Delta (Δ\DeltaΔ) Connection:

    • Line-to-line voltage equals phase voltage (VL=VϕV_L = V_\phiVL​=Vϕ​).
    • The third-harmonic voltages are in-phase and drive a circulating current around the closed Delta loop.
    • Benefit: No third-harmonic voltage exists at the terminals, but third-harmonic current circulates inside the Delta windings.

§ Distributed Winding and Breadth Factor

A simple concentrated winding produces a highly non-sinusoidal (square-like) air gap flux density containing significant odd harmonics:

B(θ)=Bs1+Bs3+Bs5+Bs7+…B(\theta) = B_{s1} + B_{s3} + B_{s5} + B_{s7} + \dots B(θ)=Bs1​+Bs3​+Bs5​+Bs7​+…

To make the flux density and induced voltage more sinusoidal, the windings are distributed across multiple slots.

  • Slot-pitch angle (lpha): The electrical angle between adjacent slots.

    α=p⋅360∘Q(electrical degrees)\alpha = \frac{p \cdot 360^\circ}{Q} \quad (\text{electrical degrees}) α=Qp⋅360∘​(electrical degrees)

  • Slots per pole per phase (qqq):

    q=Q2pmq = \frac{Q}{2pm} q=2pmQ​

    where QQQ is the total number of slots, ppp is the pole pairs, and mmm is the number of phases (m=3m=3m=3).

Breadth Factor (kdk_dkd​ or kbk_bkb​): Because the coils in adjacent slots are physically shifted by angle α\alphaα, their induced EMFs are out of phase. The vector sum of these EMFs is smaller than their algebraic sum. We define the breadth factor kdνk_{d\nu}kdν​ for the ν\nuν-th harmonic as:

kdν=sin⁡(qνα/2)qsin⁡(να/2)k_{d\nu} = \frac{\sin(q \nu \alpha / 2)}{q \sin(\nu \alpha / 2)} kdν​=qsin(να/2)sin(qνα/2)​

For the fundamental component (u=1 u = 1u=1):

kd1=sin⁡(qα/2)qsin⁡(α/2)k_{d1} = \frac{\sin(q \alpha / 2)}{q \sin(\alpha / 2)} kd1​=qsin(α/2)sin(qα/2)​

§ Double-Layer Winding and Pitch Factor

  • Double-layer winding: Each slot contains two coil sides (one top, one bottom), allowing the use of a fractional pitch (chorded winding).
  • Pitch ratio (yyy): The ratio of coil pitch to pole pitch (y=coil pitchτp≤1y = \frac{\text{coil pitch}}{\tau_p} \le 1y=τp​coil pitch​≤1). For a full-pitch coil, y=1y = 1y=1. For fractional pitch, y<1y < 1y<1.

Pitch Factor (kpk_pkp​): For a fractional-pitch coil, the coil sides are not exactly 180∘180^\circ180∘ electrical apart, reducing the induced EMF. The pitch factor kpνk_{p\nu}kpν​ for the ν\nuν-th harmonic is:

kpν=sin⁡(νyπ2)k_{p\nu} = \sin\left(\frac{\nu y \pi}{2}\right) kpν​=sin(2νyπ​)

  • Harmonic Elimination: By selecting a specific pitch ratio yyy, we can eliminate or significantly reduce certain harmonics:
    • To eliminate the 5th and 7th harmonics, we choose y=5/6≈0.83y = 5/6 \approx 0.83y=5/6≈0.83.
    • For y=5/6y = 5/6y=5/6, the fundamental pitch factor is kp1=sin⁡(5π/12)≈0.966k_{p1} = \sin(5\pi/12) \approx 0.966kp1​=sin(5π/12)≈0.966, while the 5th and 7th harmonic pitch factors are reduced to sin⁡(25π/12)≈0.259\sin(25\pi/12) \approx 0.259sin(25π/12)≈0.259 and sin⁡(35π/12)≈0.259\sin(35\pi/12) \approx 0.259sin(35π/12)≈0.259, successfully suppressing these harmonics.

§ Combined Winding Factor and Total EMF

The combined effect of distributed winding and fractional pitch is represented by the winding factor (kwk_wkw​):

kwν=kdν⋅kpνk_{w\nu} = k_{d\nu} \cdot k_{p\nu} kwν​=kdν​⋅kpν​

The total induced RMS EMF per phase for the fundamental component is:

E1=2πfNckw1Φ=4.44fNckw1ΦE_1 = \sqrt{2} \pi f N_c k_{w1} \Phi = 4.44 f N_c k_{w1} \Phi E1​=2​πfNc​kw1​Φ=4.44fNc​kw1​Φ

where NcN_cNc​ is the total number of series turns per phase:

Nc=2pqNKaN_c = \frac{2 p q N_K}{a} Nc​=a2pqNK​​

(NKN_KNK​: turns per coil, aaa: number of parallel paths).

For the ν\nuν-th harmonic:

Eν=4.44fNckwνΦνE_\nu = 4.44 f N_c k_{w\nu} \Phi_\nu Eν​=4.44fNc​kwν​Φν​

§ Armature MMF and Fourier Decomposition

When load currents flow through the stator armature windings, they generate an armature magnetomotive force (MMF).

For a single-phase winding carrying a sinusoidal current, the MMF is a pulsating standing wave:

f(x,t)=∑ν=1,3,5,…∞Fνcos⁡(νx)cos⁡(ωt)f(x, t) = \sum_{\nu=1,3,5,\dots}^{\infty} F_{\nu} \cos(\nu x) \cos(\omega t) f(x,t)=ν=1,3,5,…∑∞​Fν​cos(νx)cos(ωt)

where the peak MMF of the ν\nuν-th harmonic is:

Fν=4πNckwννImF_{\nu} = \frac{4}{\pi} \frac{N_c k_{w\nu}}{\nu} I_m Fν​=π4​νNc​kwν​​Im​

This pulsating MMF can be decomposed into two counter-rotating fields (Forward and Backward rotating fields):

f(x,t)=12Fνcos⁡(νx−ωt)+12Fνcos⁡(νx+ωt)f(x, t) = \frac{1}{2} F_{\nu} \cos(\nu x - \omega t) + \frac{1}{2} F_{\nu} \cos(\nu x + \omega t) f(x,t)=21​Fν​cos(νx−ωt)+21​Fν​cos(νx+ωt)

§ Generation of rotating magnetic field

When balanced three-phase currents flow through three-phase distributed windings, the individual phase MMFs sum up:

ftot(x,t)=fa(x,t)+fb(x,t)+fc(x,t)f_{tot}(x, t) = f_a(x, t) + f_b(x, t) + f_c(x, t) ftot​(x,t)=fa​(x,t)+fb​(x,t)+fc​(x,t)

Summing the forward and backward components for all three phases:

  • The backward-rotating components cancel each other out to zero.
  • The forward-rotating components add up constructively.

The total MMF of the fundamental component is a purely rotating magnetic field of constant magnitude:

Ftot(x,t)=32F1cos⁡(x−ωt)=32(4πNckw1Im)cos⁡(x−ωt)F_{tot}(x, t) = \frac{3}{2} F_1 \cos(x - \omega t) = \frac{3}{2} \left(\frac{4}{\pi} N_c k_{w1} I_m\right) \cos(x - \omega t) Ftot​(x,t)=23​F1​cos(x−ωt)=23​(π4​Nc​kw1​Im​)cos(x−ωt)

Ftot(x,t)=1.35Nckw1Irmscos⁡(x−ωt)F_{tot}(x, t) = 1.35 N_c k_{w1} I_{rms} \cos(x - \omega t) Ftot​(x,t)=1.35Nc​kw1​Irms​cos(x−ωt)

§ Speed and Frequency of the Rotating Field

The relationship between the electrical frequency fef_efe​ of the stator currents and the mechanical rotational speed nmn_mnm​ (rpm) of the rotating magnetic field is:

fe=P⋅nm120=p⋅fmf_e = \frac{P \cdot n_m}{120} = p \cdot f_m fe​=120P⋅nm​​=p⋅fm​

where PPP is the number of poles (p=P/2p = P/2p=P/2 is the number of pole pairs), and nmn_mnm​ is the synchronous speed.

ns=120fePn_s = \frac{120 f_e}{P} ns​=P120fe​​

  • Reversing Rotation Direction: Swap any two of the three stator phase connections (e.g. swap phase B and C). This changes the phase sequence from A−B−CA-B-CA−B−C to A−C−BA-C-BA−C−B, reversing the direction of the rotating magnetic field.

§ Space Harmonics in the MMF

For the ν\nuν-th harmonic MMF:

  • 3rd harmonic: Since the currents are 120∘120^\circ120∘ out of phase in time, the sum of third-harmonic MMFs is zero. There is no rotating 3rd-harmonic MMF.
  • 5th harmonic: Decomposing the 5th harmonic shows that it forms a negative-sequence rotating field. It rotates in the opposite direction (backward) at 1/51/51/5 of the synchronous speed:

    n5=−ns5n_5 = -\frac{n_s}{5} n5​=−5ns​​

  • 7th harmonic: Forms a positive-sequence rotating field. It rotates in the same direction (forward) at 1/71/71/7 of the synchronous speed:

    n7=+ns7n_7 = +\frac{n_s}{7} n7​=+7ns​​

§ Induced Torque and Power Flow

The interaction between the stator-produced magnetic field B⃗S\vec{B}_SBS​ and the rotor-produced magnetic field B⃗R\vec{B}_RBR​ generates an electromagnetic torque:

τ⃗ind=kB⃗R×B⃗S\vec{\tau}_{ind} = k \vec{B}_R \times \vec{B}_S τind​=kBR​×BS​

τind=kBRBSsin⁡δ\tau_{ind} = k B_R B_S \sin\delta τind​=kBR​BS​sinδ

where δ\deltaδ is the power angle (torque angle) between the two fields.

Power Flow in AC Machines:

  • Losses:

    1. Stator copper loss (3-phase): PSCL=3IA2RAP_{SCL} = 3 I_A^2 R_APSCL​=3IA2​RA​
    2. Rotor copper loss: PRCL=IR2RRP_{RCL} = I_R^2 R_RPRCL​=IR2​RR​
    3. Core losses (PcoreP_{core}Pcore​): Hysteresis and eddy currents in the stator core.
    4. Mechanical losses: Friction and windage.
    5. Stray losses: Miscellaneous load losses.
