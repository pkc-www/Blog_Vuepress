Idealized Characteristics

An ideal power switch, with on-state current iTi_TiT​ and off-state voltage vTv_TvT​:

  1. Can be turned on and/or off by a control signal;
  2. Zero gate firing power;
  3. When on, iTi_TiT​ flows only in the given direction;
  4. Zero conduction and switching losses (Von-state=0V_{on\text{-}state} = 0Von-state​=0, Ileak=0I_{leak} = 0Ileak​=0);
  5. Unlimited iTi_TiT​ and vTv_TvT​ ratings;
  6. Instant switching;
  7. Low cost.

Rated Power vs. Switching Frequency

重要

No free lunch! Devices with a high power rating (thick drift layers, lots of stored charge) switch slowly, while fast devices have lower power ratings. Choosing a device is always a trade-off between power and switching frequency.

1-1 Power Diode

Output characteristics: Von-state≈1∼3 VV_{on\text{-}state} \approx 1\sim3\ \text{V}Von-state​≈1∼3 V, IleakageI_{leakage}Ileakage​ is negligible.

Most power diodes are PiN diodes: iii is a lightly doped n−n^-n− epitaxial layer (low carrier density) sandwiched between p+p^+p+ and n+n^+n+.

Why PiN: Low Forward Voltage Drop, High Power

According to Poisson's equation

∇2φ=−ρε\nabla^2 \varphi = -\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon} ∇2φ=−ερ​

the field is linear in a doped depletion region and flat in the nearly intrinsic iii layer. For the same voltage VVV (the area under ∣E(x)∣|E(x)|∣E(x)∣), the field of a PN junction is triangular, while that of a PiN diode is trapezoidal, so

Emax′<EmaxE'_{max} < E_{max} Emax′​<Emax​

Breakdown happens when the peak field reaches the critical field EcE_cEc​, so a PiN diode can block a much higher voltage for the same peak field.

★ Conductivity Modulation

A thick, lightly doped n−n^-n− layer would normally mean a large resistance. Under forward bias, however, holes are injected from p+p^+p+ and electrons from n+n^+n+, and their density in the n−n^-n− region becomes far higher than the background doping. The on-state resistance is highly reduced due to carrier injection.

Both electrons and holes take part in conduction, so the device is bipolar.

注

The price of conductivity modulation is the stored charge in the n−n^-n− region: it has to be built up at turn-on and removed at turn-off. Every bipolar device (diode, SCR, BJT, IGBT) pays this price in switching speed.

Typical Diode Switching Transient

Turn-on: conductivity modulation needs time to establish, so the diode transiently has a high resistance and v(t)v(t)v(t) shows a forward-recovery overshoot before settling to the low on-state drop. The on-state current itself is determined by the converter circuit.

Turn-off:

  • Interval (4): the current reverses but the diode remains forward-biased while the stored charge in the n−n^-n− region is removed.
  • Interval (5): the diode becomes reverse-biased and the depletion-region capacitance charges, so the reverse current decays to zero.

1-3 Power Transistor (GTR/BJT)

A power BJT (giant transistor, GTR) is normally an NPN device with an n−n^-n− drift layer in the collector to block high voltage. It is current-controlled: in the active region IC=βIBI_C = \beta I_BIC​=βIB​.

  • As a switch it works between cut-off (IB=0I_B = 0IB​=0) and saturation (IB>IC/βI_B > I_C/\betaIB​>IC​/β), avoiding the active region where vCEiCv_{CE} i_CvCE​iC​ is large.
  • It is bipolar: the drift region is conductivity-modulated in (quasi-)saturation, so VCE(sat)V_{CE(sat)}VCE(sat)​ is low.
  • The current gain of a power BJT is small (β≈5∼10\beta \approx 5\sim10β≈5∼10), so a continuous, large base current is needed. Darlington connections are often used.
  • Stored charge makes it slow to turn off, and it suffers from second breakdown.

Today it has largely been replaced by the MOSFET and the IGBT.

1-4 Power MOSFET

N-channel enhancement type, with three terminals: Drain, Gate and Source.

When VGSV_{GS}VGS​ exceeds the threshold voltage VGS(th)V_{GS(th)}VGS(th)​, electrons are attracted to the surface of the PPP body under the gate oxide and form an inversion layer. This turns the PPP region between N+N^+N+ (source) and N−N^-N− (drift) into an equivalent NNN channel, so the device acts as an NNN conductor.

  • Carrier: electron (unipolar).

提示

The PPP body and N−N^-N− drift region form an intrinsic body diode, so a MOSFET can always conduct reverse current. Bridge circuits often use it as the freewheeling diode.

There is no conductivity modulation, so R↑R \uparrowR↑ (unipolar). The drift-region resistance rises steeply with the blocking voltage, roughly RDS(on)∝VBR2.5R_{DS(on)} \propto V_{BR}^{2.5}RDS(on)​∝VBR2.5​.

  • Advantages: high frequency (no stored minority charge); voltage-controlled with very small gate drive power; positive temperature coefficient of RDS(on)R_{DS(on)}RDS(on)​, so devices are easy to parallel.
  • Disadvantages: high on-resistance at high voltage, low power rating.

1-5 IGBT (Insulated Gate Bipolar Transistor)

Structure from collector to emitter: P+/N+/N−/P/N+P^+ / N^+ / N^- / P / N^+P+/N+/N−/P/N+. It is a MOSFET with an extra P+P^+P+ injection layer on the collector side:

  • The MOS channel supplies the base current of a PNP transistor (P+N−PP^+ N^- PP+N−P), so the gate is voltage-controlled like a MOSFET.
  • Holes injected from P+P^+P+ conductivity-modulate the N−N^-N− region, so the on-state drop is low like a BJT and the device handles high voltage and high current.
  • At turn-off the stored holes cause a tail current, so an IGBT is slower than a MOSFET.

警告

  • A parasitic NPN (N+PN−N^+ P N^-N+PN−) together with the PNP forms a thyristor. If it latches up, the gate loses control; the PPP-body shorting resistor in the equivalent circuit is designed to prevent this.
  • The P+P^+P+ collector blocks reverse current, so unlike a MOSFET there is no body diode: an external anti-parallel diode is packaged with it.
